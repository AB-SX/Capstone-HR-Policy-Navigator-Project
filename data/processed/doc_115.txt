Succession Planning Process
The succession planning process might vary slightly for different areas of the business depending
on size and structure, but at a high level the process should flow as follows:
Leaders go through the Performance/Growth Potential Assessment process for all
of their direct reports. Ideally, this process includes calibration to generate a level of
initial familiarity amongst leadership peers with the talent in the organization.
Leaders identify and document their respective successors (and emergency
coverage plans if the identified successor could not currently cover in an emergency
situation) leveraging this template.
Succession plans are reviewed with peers (i.e. all other executives, all other senior
leaders in the function, etc.) and your People Business Partner, ideally during a
synchronous session to facilitate discussion and questions.
Development plans are finalized for successors and documented in the successor
slide.
(if applicable) Skills gaps impacting ability to achieve business goals are identified
and actions are put in place to ensure these gaps are closed/minimized (i.e. stretch
assignments, increased visibility and exposure, or hiring)

Key Talent
Key Talent Criteria
Key Talent makes up roughly ~10% of the population. Key Talent represents team members who
have a significant impact on GitLab’s Key Talent represents approximately 10% of our workforce—
team members who consistently exceed performance expectations in their current role AND
demonstrate strong potential for greater scope and responsibility. They deliver measurable
results that advance critical company initiatives while aligning with our values. Team members at
any level can be Key Talent.
Characteristics of Key Talent
Team members identified as Key Talent:
Consistently surpass performance expectations with valuable contributions beyond
requirements
Substantially impact the company’s ability to achieve significant milestones
Demonstrate growth potential and desire to assume broader or more complex
responsibilities
Has an outsized, measurable impact on business performance
Significantly impact critical functional areas
Has demonstrated the ability to scale with the organization
Key Talent, Performance, and Growth Potential
Key Talent should typically be assessed as “Exceeding and Growing” or higher (Boxes 1, 2, or 3). In
rare cases, “Performing and Growing” (Box 4) may qualify with strong justification showing
exceptional impact AND clear trajectory toward exceeding expectations. Box 4 should be the
exception, not the norm.
Team members with less than 3 months tenure (“TNTR” - Too New to Rate) cannot be considered
Key Talent.
Examples of Key Talent:
A Staff Engineer who consistently exceeds delivery expectations while driving
architectural decisions across multiple teams, led platform improvements reducing
system latency by 40%, established technical standards adopted across engineering,
mentored three engineers into senior roles, and proactively solved scaling challenges.
An Account Executive consistently achieving 130-150% of quota while expanding deal
sizes by 35% year-over-year, closed strategic reference accounts contributing to
enterprise market expansion, developed a discovery methodology now used
organization-wide, and mentored two junior AEs who exceeded ramp targets.
A Senior Manager consistently delivering above team targets while completing two
director-level stretch assignments, built scalable processes improving team efficiency by
25%, and developed two direct reports who were promoted.
