Denmark Statutory Leave Policies
Below is the information related to Denmark’s statutory leave policies. Please note that team

Denmark Holiday Leave
All full-time workers are entitled to 5 weeks (25 days) paid holiday leave a year under the
Holiday Act (Lov om ferie).
The holiday year runs from 1 September to 31 August, and any accrued holidays can be
taken until 31 December of the following year, allowing a total period of 16 months to
utilise the leave.
Denmark Pregnancy, Maternity and Paternity Leave
In total, parents in Denmark get 52 weeks of paid parental leave. The leave entitlement is
structured as follows:
Maternity Leave: For Mothers
4 weeks of pregnancy leave: Can be taken up to 4 weeks before the due date, with
maternity/paternity benefits.
2 weeks after birth: Must be taken immediately after birth and cannot be transferred to
the other parent.
8 weeks at birth: Must be taken within the first 10 weeks after the child is born; can be
extended if returning to part-time work. This leave can be transferred to the other
parent if they meet the conditions for receiving maternity/paternity benefits.
9 weeks of leave: Cannot be transferred; must be taken within 1 year of the child’s birth
unless prevented by special circumstances.
5 weeks of leave: Must be taken within 1 year, but can be extended or postponed until
the child reaches age 9 if conditions are met. This leave can be transferred to the other
parent if they meet the conditions for receiving maternity/paternity benefits.
Total for mothers: 4 weeks pregnancy + 24 weeks maternity leave (with 13 weeks transferable
under certain conditions)
Paternity Leave: For Fathers or Co-Mothers
2 weeks at birth: Must be taken within the first 2 weeks after birth, and can be taken
flexibly over the first 10 weeks with employer agreement. The 2 weeks of leave at birth
can be taken as individual days or split over the 10-week period. The leave corresponds
to the employee’s weekly working hours (e.g., full-time 37 hours/week = 74 hours total
for the 2 weeks).
9 weeks of leave: Cannot be transferred; must be taken within 1 year unless special
circumstances prevent it.
13 weeks of leave: Must be taken within 1 year, but can be extended or postponed until
the child reaches age 9 if conditions are met. This leave can be transferred to the other
parent if conditions for maternity/paternity benefits are met at the time of the leave.
