The shadow and ramp framework
No amount of reading prepares you for running a customer call. The shadow and ramp
framework gives you a structured
path from observing to leading, with feedback at every stage.

The timing is a guide, not a deadline. You move to the next stage when your buddy and manager
agree you are ready.
If you need more time in a stage, ask for it.
Observer. Shadowing is not passive. Before the call, ask your buddy what type of call it
is, what the customer’s
context is, and what a good outcome looks like. During the call, notice the structure as
well as the content: how
your buddy opens, moves between topics, handles questions they cannot answer, and
closes with clear next steps.
Co-lead. Your buddy tells the customer up front that you will cover a topic. Pick
something you can prepare
thoroughly, aim for 10 to 15 minutes, and brief your buddy beforehand so they can step
in cleanly if needed.
Reverse shadow. This is the most important stage. Your buddy is visible on the invite
but says nothing unless
there is a factual error or a genuine emergency. Prepare exactly as you would for a solo
call.
Solo lead. Spot checks continue throughout your time on the team; every CSE has their
calls reviewed from time
to time. If a solo call goes sideways, debrief it anyway and bring it to your next one-to-
one.

Post-call debrief template
Use it after every shadow session, whatever the stage. Fill it in straight after the call, while the
detail is
fresh. It takes five minutes.
What was the customer’s primary goal coming into the call? Not what was on the
agenda: what did they actually
want?
What technique or approach did you observe, or use, that you want to adopt? Be
specific.
What would you have done differently, and why? From stage 2 onwards, focus on
your own segment or lead.
What follow-up was committed to, and who owns it? If ownership is unclear, that is a
learning point in itself.
How feedback is structured
After co-lead and reverse shadow sessions, feedback covers four areas. Knowing them in advance
means you can assess yourself first.
Value articulation: did you connect GitLab to the customer’s stated goals and business
context?
Technical accuracy: were your recommendations correct, current and practical, and
within CSE scope? When you
were unsure, did you commit to follow up rather than guess?
Relationship and risk management: did you read the room, notice when something
the customer said was worth
probing, and manage the pace of the call?
Follow-through: were the next steps clear, owned and realistic, and did you set
expectations for the follow-up
email?
Webinars
The same progression applies to webinars, with one change: you start as co-pilot, working the
chat and Q&A while
another CSE presents, usually from Module 2. Your first solo presentation typically comes after
your first successful
reverse shadow, around Module 4. See webinars and hands-on labs.
