Attendance and/or recording completion is mandatory and tracked as part of Academy
measurement.
Component 3: Role-Based Skills Development and Shadowing
New hires must:
Attend all role-based skills workshops scheduled for their cohort and role (AE, xDR, SA,
CX, etc.), or complete the full recording and associated exercises when live attendance is
not possible and their manager has approved the exception.
Complete assigned shadowing and field practice, using the guided shadow plan with
their manager and buddy to apply Academy learning in real scenarios.
This component focuses on practical application (discovery, demos, pipeline generation, success
planning, forecasting, etc.) and is a key input into the manager’s view of field-readiness.
Component 4: In-Person Graduation Summit and Stand-and-Deliver Assessment
New hires are expected to:
Attend their assigned in-person Academy Graduation Summit, a 2 to 2.5 day regional
event (AMER, EMEA, APJ) featuring cross-functional workshops and role-specific
certification activities.
Participate fully in the stand-and-deliver capstone assessment, demonstrating their
ability to apply the GitLab value selling methodology in a realistic prospect or customer
scenario.
Achieve a “Pass” rating on the stand-and-deliver rubric for their role.
The formal checkpoint for this component is: “Attend the in-person event and pass the role-
specific capstone assessment.”
Default rule: Attending the regional Summit and passing the stand-and-deliver assessment is
required to graduate from GitLab Revenue Academy.
2. How Graduation Is Assessed
Data Sources Used in Graduation Decisions
Academy graduation decisions are supported by:
Course completion and knowledge checks (Component 1)
Attendance and/or recording completion for CoM and role-based skills workshops
(Components 2–3)
Shadowing and on-the-job practice and application between the new hire, manager,
and onboarding buddy (Components 1–3)
Performance in the stand-and-deliver capstone assessment during the in-person
Graduation Summit (Component 4)
These inputs are consolidated into Academy progress reporting and shared with hiring managers
to provide clearer readiness signals and make coaching and forecasting easier.

Stand-and-Deliver Assessment Outcomes
The stand-and-deliver capstone is scored using a standardized rubric tailored to each role group.
While the exact rubric may vary, outcomes will be recorded at a minimum as:
Merit — The new hire meets all Pass criteria and significantly exceeds expectations on
key dimensions (e.g., exceptional clarity of value narrative, advanced objection handling,
and strong executive presence). Merit can be used to recognize top performers and is
still considered a full Pass for graduation purposes.
Pass — The new hire demonstrates field-ready mastery of the Academy learning
objectives for their role (e.g., clear value narrative, effective discovery, differentiation,
and linking to customer outcomes).
Needs Development — The new hire has meaningful gaps in applying the methodology
or role-specific skills in a prospect or customer scenario and requires additional
coaching, practice, or re-assessment before graduation.
Role-based exercises may apply varying score bands to the rubric (e.g., Merit = ≥90, Pass = 80–
89, Needs Development <80). Merit and Pass are both treated as “passing” outcomes for
graduation, with Merit serving to highlight top performers.
3. Manager Discretion and Exceptions
Manager’s Role in Graduation Decisions
While Academy provides the structured program, checkpoints, rubrics, and reporting, hiring
managers remain accountable for role readiness and core onboarding, including the new
hire onboarding issue, buddy assignment, and protecting time for Academy work.
Consistent with our program design, passing criteria for the Academy (including the stand-
and-deliver panel assessment) are ultimately at hiring manager discretion, within
standardized guidance and this framework.
Managers should use Academy data as the default signal, then apply judgment based on:
The new hire’s actual performance in the field
Timing and logistics constraints (e.g., illness, travel issues, regional conflicts)
The broader context of ramp expectations for their role and region
Default Expectation vs. Exceptions
Default expectation
By default, a new hire graduates when all of the following are true:
Component 1: Required GitLab Foundations and role-based eLearning courses are
completed and knowledge checks are passed by the Week 3 checkpoint (or within an
agreed extension window).
Component 2: CoM / methodology class is fully completed (live sessions and/or
approved recordings) within the cohort’s schedule.
Component 3: Role-based skills workshops and required shadowing are completed with
active participation.
