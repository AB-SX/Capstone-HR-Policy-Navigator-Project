Component 4: The new hire attends the in-person Graduation Summit and earns a
“Pass” (or “Merit”) rating on the stand-and-deliver assessment.
Manager-approved exceptions
Managers may exercise discretion when:
The new hire misses a live virtual session but completes the full recording and any
associated exercises within the required timeframe, with written manager approval.
There are minor gaps in LMS progress (e.g., one or two overdue micro-courses) but the
manager has strong evidence of field-ready performance in the field.
There are meaningful gaps in required coursework (e.g., multiple missing modules or
knowledge checks) — the expectation is that they do not attend an Academy Summit.
They should first complete a targeted make-up plan (see “Handling Below-Passing
Performance or Non-Completion”) before attendance is approved. In edge cases where
the manager believes the new hire is clearly field-ready despite these gaps, attendance
may be approved only with a documented exception and clear remediation plan.
The new hire’s assessment score is at or just below the formal passing threshold,
but panel feedback and manager observations indicate they are performing at the
expected level in actual prospect or customer conversations.
In these cases, the manager may approve graduation, paired with a targeted coaching plan
(e.g., additional practice sessions, deal reviews, or focused shadowing).
Handling Below-Passing Performance or Non-Completion
When a new hire does not meet the Academy requirements — for example:
Significant portions of the eLearning path are incomplete (including cases where
Summit attendance was approved and the stand-and-deliver result is “Pass” but required
learning is still missing)
Multiple required workshops are missed without approved make-ups
The stand-and-deliver result is “Needs Development”
There are ongoing concerns about field-readiness despite Academy participation
…the expectation is that the new hire does not graduate yet and instead follows a remediation
and re-assessment path, owned by the hiring manager and supported by Enablement.
Recommended options include:
1. Make-up coursework and workshops
Complete all missing asynchronous courses and knowledge checks.
Attend future live skills workshops (or complete the recording and exercises) to close
gaps specific to their role.
2. Re-attending an in-person Graduation Summit
In some cases, the manager may determine that the new hire should repeat the in-
person Summit to re-do the stand-and-deliver exercises and benefit from the full in-
person experience before granting graduation.

3. Virtual stand-and-deliver re-assessment
For cases where waiting for the next in-person Summit is not practical, the manager can
schedule a virtual stand-and-deliver assessment using the same rubric, with a panel
that includes at least the hiring manager and an Enablement Lead or Enablement
Consultant.
A “Pass” on this virtual re-assessment can satisfy the capstone requirement for
graduation.
Decisions between these paths should factor in role expectations, timing, and regional Summit
cadence (AMER/EMEA approximately every other month, APJ quarterly).
Documentation and Communication of Exceptions
To maintain consistency and transparency, all exceptions (graduating with partial completion,
virtual re-assessment, or requiring a re-do at a future Summit) should be documented by the
hiring manager, including:
Which requirements were not met
Rationale for the decision (e.g., demonstrated field performance, unavoidable conflict,
health issues)
Any required remediation or follow-up plan
Managers should share exception decisions with their regional/functional VP/AVP/Director and
Enablement Lead so we maintain consistent standards across cohorts and roles.
This approach preserves standardized graduation gates and accountability while honoring
manager judgment on whether a new hire is truly field-ready at the end of Academy.
